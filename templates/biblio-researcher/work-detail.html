Abstract

Restored tidal marshes along the Scheldt accumulate nitrogen and phosphorus at rates that differ sharply from reference marshes in the first decade after breaching. This study tracks nutrient stocks across eleven sites over four growing seasons and relates them to sediment supply and inundation frequency.
